AI heralds a profound change in every scientific discipline. Mathematics is already experiencing it head-on, and physics is now beginning to as well.

We think this is the right moment to reflect together on how AI will transform the way we do physics — from theory to experiment — and also the way we publish, hire and train; and on how to use it well, keeping our role as researchers at the centre. These questions matter for researchers, and even more for students, PhD candidates and early-career researchers, whose training and careers will unfold in this new context.

## Three strands

The seminar is organised around three kinds of talks:

- **Perspectives** — overviews of how AI could transform the discipline, its practices and education.
- **Results** — the physics content of new results obtained with AI, and how AI was used to obtain them.
- **Methods** — how to use AI well in physics, for researchers, PhD candidates and students.

## How to take part

Sessions take different formats — talks followed by an open discussion, round tables, series of informal presentations — and are held in room Conf. IV at the Laboratoire de Physique of the ENS, 24 rue Lhomond, Paris. They are open to everyone. Announcements are sent to the [mailing list]({{ site.baseurl }}/subscribe/), and slides, recordings and notes are collected under [Resources]({{ site.baseurl }}/resources/). Suggestions for speakers and topics are welcome: write to the organisers.
